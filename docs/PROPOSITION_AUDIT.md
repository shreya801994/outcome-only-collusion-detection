# Adversarial audit of PROPOSITION_v2 (2 Nov 2026)

**Scope.** No experiments were run. The checks are numerical and symbolic-by-hand only (`experiments/audit_propositions.py`). No historical result, threshold or pre-registration changed. The corrected text is `docs/PROPOSITION_v3.md`.

## 1. Actual mathematical errors in v2

| Location in v2 | Error | Severity | Fixed in v3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proposition 3, proof | "Prices are generated by the same rule from the same inputs" is asserted, not proved. Identical inputs require (i) a policy whose state excludes anything computed from true cost (realised profit, reward, learning state) and (ii) coupled randomness. Neither was stated. The proof is **incomplete** in general. It is valid for our simulator, which satisfies both (code below). | **High** for any claim about learning agents; none for our results | Conditions (S) and (C); induction proof; failure case stated |
| Proposition 3, statement | Admissibility of the twin's costs (c + m ∈ 𝒞) appeared only as an "unless" clause | Low | Made a hypothesis |

No algebraic error was found:
- the derivatives;

## 2. Missing assumptions (now stated in v3)
- **Attractiveness domain.** a ∈ ℝ³, unrestricted. Needed for the inversion âᵢ = pᵢ + μ ln(sᵢ/s₀) + a₀.
- **Shares, payoffs and prices.** Interior shares, Q > 0, no capacity constraints, prices unrestricted on ℝ (the simulator clips to [0.5, 4]).
- **Stationary repeated game.** Proposition 1(ii) uses a fixed stage game; it is not a statement about the shock environments.
- **Proposition 2(b): global optimality.** v2 derived only the necessary first-order condition while describing "the joint-profit price". v3 adds **Lemma 2**, which proves existence, uniqueness and global optimality of the joint-profit maximiser with equal markups μ/s₀. The proof is a change of variables: a strictly concave inner problem at fixed X, then a one-dimensional problem with a single crossing. It is checked numerically on five configurations, including asymmetric ones. A verified literature citation is still to be found.
- **Proposition 2(b): constancy.** "Varies whenever shares vary" is replaced by an exact if-and-only-if condition on sⱼ/(s₀(1 − sᵢ)).
- **Proposition 3:** conditions (S) state, (C) coupling, and c + m ∈ 𝒞.

## 5. Claims that must be removed from the paper
1. Any statement that Proposition 3 holds for **learning agents** (Q-learning, bandits, LLMs) without specifying that their reward is computed with the padded cost.
2. Calling the cost-mimicking policy a "cartel", "collusive equilibrium" or "sustainable tacit collusion".
3. "Competition implies constant implied cost." Replace with "contemporaneous static Nash at constant cost implies constant implied cost."
4. "Share-moving shocks necessarily destabilise cartel implied costs." Replace with the if-and-only-if conditions of Proposition 2(b)-(c).
5. Proposition 2(b) applied to the tracking cartel (κ < 1).
6. Proposition 1 presented as evidence about actual costs, or as rationalising a whole non-stationary path with one cost vector.
7. **"Non-identification"** used for the AUC of 0.854, the AUC of 0.61 in E0, the 100% FPR at a frozen threshold, or the μ̂-misspecification FPRs. These are finite-sample discrimination, calibration failure or distribution shift (v3 table).
8. "Adjustment speed cannot be identified." It is latent and possibly estimable; this is untested (Option B, unrun).
9. Any claim of uniqueness of the **duopoly Nash equilibrium**. Only best-response uniqueness (Lemma 1) and joint-profit uniqueness (Lemma 2) are proved.
10. Citing Mizuno (2003), or Lau (1982) as a logit-Bertrand result.
11. Any of Propositions 1-3 presented as a novel identification theorem. Proposition 1 overlaps with Belal (2026), and Proposition 2 is Bresnahan-style logic.
