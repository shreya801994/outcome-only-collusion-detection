# Formal results (draft for supervisor review)

Status: proof sketches checked numerically (tests in `tests/test_equilibrium.py`, `tests/test_implied_cost.py`, `tests/test_batch_sim.py`). They are applications of classic conduct identification (Bresnahan 1982; Lau 1982), stated for this model. They are **not** claimed as new theory.

## Model
- **Players and timing:** two firms i ∈ {1, 2}, periods t = 1, …, T.
- **Demand:** multinomial logit with an outside good:

  sᵢ(p; a) = exp((aᵢ − pᵢ)/μ) / [exp(a₀/μ) + Σⱼ exp((aⱼ − pⱼ)/μ)],  μ > 0.

  Quantities are qᵢ = Q·sᵢ. Static profit is πᵢ = (pᵢ − cᵢ)·Q·sᵢ, with constant marginal cost cᵢ.
- **The auditor observes:** prices, quantities and, where stated, Q (equivalently the outside share s₀). It knows μ, a₀ and the attractiveness levels only where stated. It never observes cᵢ.

## Lemma 1 (unique best response)
For any rival price pⱼ, own profit has a unique maximiser on p > cᵢ, characterised by pᵢ − cᵢ = μ / (1 − sᵢ).

*Proof.*
1. For p ≤ cᵢ profit is ≤ 0, while any p > cᵢ gives positive profit. So a maximiser lies in (cᵢ, ∞).
2. There, log πᵢ = log(p − cᵢ) + log sᵢ + log Q.
3. Using ∂sᵢ/∂pᵢ = −sᵢ(1 − sᵢ)/μ gives ∂ log sᵢ/∂pᵢ = −(1 − sᵢ)/μ, and so ∂² log sᵢ/∂pᵢ² = −sᵢ(1 − sᵢ)/μ² < 0.
4. Both terms of log πᵢ are strictly concave, so log πᵢ is strictly concave and its stationary point is the unique global maximiser.
5. Setting ∂ log πᵢ/∂pᵢ = 1/(p − cᵢ) − (1 − sᵢ)/μ = 0 gives the condition. ∎

(The closed form uses the Lambert W function; see `simulator/equilibrium.py`.)

## Proposition 1 (stationary observational equivalence)
Fix the demand parameters (a, a₀, μ). Let (p₁, p₂) be any price pair with shares sᵢ = sᵢ(p; a), and define

ĉᵢ = pᵢ − μ / (1 − sᵢ).

If ĉᵢ lies in the auditor's admissible cost set 𝒞 (for example ĉᵢ ≥ 0) for both firms, then:
1. (p₁, p₂) is a static Nash equilibrium of the game with costs (ĉ₁, ĉ₂);
2. the stationary path "play (p₁, p₂) every period" is a subgame-perfect equilibrium of the repeated game, for any discount factor.

So a stationary outcome generated by collusion at true costs c is observationally identical to competition at costs ĉ.

*Proof.*
1. By Lemma 1, pᵢ is firm i's unique best response to pⱼ at cost ĉᵢ, because the first-order condition holds by construction and ĉᵢ < pᵢ automatically.
2. Hence (p₁, p₂) is a Nash equilibrium. **Uniqueness is not claimed and not needed:** observational equivalence only requires that competition at ĉ *can* produce the outcome. (Revised after the final audit, `docs/FINAL_AUDIT.md` §3.)
3. Repeating a static Nash equilibrium is subgame perfect. ∎

*Example.* The collusive outcome (1.90, 1.90) at true cost 1.00 has share 0.3745, so ĉ = 1.5003. It is a Nash equilibrium at cost 1.5003 (verified numerically, `audit/stage_a/a02_market_checks.py`).

**What it does not show.**
- It does not say outcome-only detection is impossible. It says stationary outcomes alone cannot separate conduct unless the admissible cost set 𝒞 excludes ĉ.
- A known cost bound is exactly what makes a price-level screen work, and B0's failures under hidden costs (FPR 0.57 with a naive threshold) are the empirical side of this.

## Proposition 2 (share variation separates fixed-markup cartels from constant-cost competition)
Let attractiveness aₜ vary over time while costs stay constant, and let the auditor observe shares sᵢₜ (for example, quantities and Q). Then:

- **(a) Competition:** under static Nash pricing each period, ĉᵢₜ = cᵢ for all t. The implied cost is constant.
- **(b) Joint-profit cartel:** under joint-profit maximisation, the common markup is pᵢ − cᵢ = μ/s₀, so

  ĉᵢₜ = cᵢ + μ·sⱼₜ / (s₀ₜ·(1 − sᵢₜ)).

  This varies whenever shares vary.
- **(c) Rigid cartel:** under prices fixed at p̄, ĉᵢₜ = p̄ᵢ − μ/(1 − sᵢₜ), which varies with sᵢₜ.

So any variation in (sᵢₜ, s₀ₜ) that moves these expressions makes "constant implied cost" a testable restriction that separates (a) from (b) and (c). This is the logit analogue of Bresnahan's demand-rotation argument.

*Proof.* Substitute the respective first-order conditions into ĉ = p − μ/(1 − s). ∎

## Proposition 3 (the boundary: cost-mimicking conduct is never identified)
Let a cartel price, in every period and every demand state, at the static Nash prices of the game with costs cᵢ + mᵢ, where mᵢ > 0 is constant. Then for every demand path its outcome process equals that of competitive firms with true costs cᵢ + mᵢ. No screen that uses only prices, quantities, market size and demand parameters can distinguish the two, whatever the demand variation, unless the admissible cost set excludes cᵢ + mᵢ.

*Proof.*
1. The outcome process is a function of (prices, demand), and the prices are identical by construction.
2. Numerically, `experiments/v2_check_padding_identity.py` finds a maximum difference of 0.0 in prices and quantities under shared random streams. ∎

## Remarks for the paper
1. **What D tests.** D (implied-cost stability) tests consistency with *constant-cost, immediately adjusting static competition*. It detects departures from that model, not collusion as such.
   - Addendum 2, H1: slow competitors are flagged at a 100% rate under a threshold calibrated on fast competitors.
   - H2: a cost-mimicking cartel goes undetected (AUC 0.38).
2. **Information requirements.**
   - Shares are needed. Quantities without market size give D's AUC 0.81 instead of 1.00 under market-size shocks.
   - μ needs to be known only roughly: ±20% leaves D's AUC at 1.00 under share shocks.
   - Knowledge of the attractiveness shocks is not needed.
3. **Assumptions stated explicitly.** Logit demand, constant marginal cost, known (or roughly known) μ, static best-response benchmark, no capacity constraints. Outside these assumptions the implied-cost relation need not hold.
